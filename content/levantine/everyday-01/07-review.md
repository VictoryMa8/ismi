# 7. Put it together

**Unpublished AI-assisted draft · owner review required · no native-expert review**

Goal: Follow a new exchange about the day and family, then agree on food, a drink, and a time.

A fresh Friday check-in: Fattoush has already visited her mother. She and Knafeh plan a quiet meal for tomorrow at seven. Use the new details rather than memorized answers.

## Dialogue / recording script

**Knafeh → Fattoush**

كيف كان يومك؟

Kīf kān yōmik?

How was your day?

**Fattoush**

كان منيح. زرت أمي وبعدين ارتحت.

Kān mnīḥ. Zurt immi w-baʿdēn irtaḥt.

It was good. I visited my mother and then rested.

**Knafeh**

وكيفها؟

W-kīfha?

And how is she?

**Fattoush → Knafeh**

منيحة. شو رأيك ناكل سوا بكرا؟

Mnīḥa. Shū raʾyak nākol sawa bukra?

She’s well. What do you think about eating together tomorrow?

**Knafeh**

تمام. نعمل رز وسلطة الساعة سبعة؟

Tamām. Niʿmal ruzz w-salaṭa is-sāʿa sabʿa?

Okay. Shall we make rice and salad at seven?

**Fattoush**

آه، وبعدين نشرب شاي.

Āh, w-baʿdēn nishrab shāy.

Yes, and then we’ll drink tea.

## Teaching note

Follow the changing reference point: kīf kān yōmik asks about Fattoush’s day; kīfha then refers to her mother, not Fattoush. The conversation moves from completed actions to a suggestion with bukra. A useful response must preserve who, when, and what happened. This lesson combines familiar pieces in a different situation. First read the Arabic and transliteration with the English covered. Explain the story to yourself, then check the English only where you need it. Complete the closing exchange without looking back if you can; hints and unlimited retries remain available. This is guided selection practice, not a test of spontaneous speaking or evidence of fluency.

Urban Palestinian teaching target. Yōmik addresses Fattoush, while raʾyak addresses Knafeh in Fattoush’s invitation. Describing yōm with mnīḥ stays masculine even though Fattoush is speaking. The past first-person forms do not change with the speaker’s gender. The shared future plan is clear from bukra. Transliteration: ā/ī/ū and ē/ō mark long vowels; ʿ = ع, ḥ = ح, ʾ = glottal stop, sh = ش, kh = خ, gh = غ. Doubled letters are held longer. Jordanian alternatives are not introduced in this unit.

## Practice and closing exchange

### 1. Fattoush says her day was good, then names a visit followed by rest. Choose her account.

كيف كان يومك؟

Kīf kān yōmik?

How was your day? (to Fattoush)

- **A** اشتغلت، وبعدين زرت أمي. — Ishtaghalt, w-baʿdēn zurt immi. — I worked, then visited my mother.
  Rationale: This adds work and omits rest; it recalls an earlier lesson rather than this new scene.
- **B (accepted)** كان منيح. زرت أمي وبعدين ارتحت. — Kān mnīḥ. Zurt immi w-baʿdēn irtaḥt. — It was good. I visited my mother and then rested.
  Rationale: This preserves both her positive check-in and the visit-before-rest sequence.
- **C** كان منيح. ارتحت وبعدين زرت أمي. — Kān mnīḥ. Irtaḥt w-baʿdēn zurt immi. — It was good. I rested and then visited my mother.
  Rationale: This reverses the two events.

Retry hint: Check the order of the visit and rest.

### 2. Knafeh’s follow-up refers to Fattoush’s mother. Answer about her, not yourself.

وكيفها؟

W-kīfha?

And how is she?

- **A** أنا منيحة. — Ana mnīḥa. — I’m well. (Fattoush speaking)
  Rationale: Ana changes the answer to Fattoush’s own condition.
- **B** منيحين، شكراً. — Mnīḥīn, shukran. — They’re well, thank you.
  Rationale: The plural answer is about several people, not one mother.
- **C (accepted)** منيحة. — Mnīḥa. — She’s well.
  Rationale: The mother is the most recently named woman. The answer stays about her.

Retry hint: Keep the person referred to by -ha.

### 3. Move from the completed day to an invitation for tomorrow. Address Knafeh.

منيحة.

Mnīḥa.

She’s well.

- **A (accepted)** شو رأيك ناكل سوا بكرا؟ — Shū raʾyak nākol sawa bukra? — What do you think about eating together tomorrow? (to Knafeh)
  Rationale: This changes to a shared future meal and keeps Knafeh as the listener.
- **B** شو عملت اليوم؟ — Shū ʿmilt il-yōm? — What did you do today? (to Knafeh)
  Rationale: This asks for another past activity report rather than inviting him.
- **C** شو رأيك ناكل سوا اليوم؟ — Shū raʾyak nākol sawa il-yōm? — What do you think about eating together today? (to Knafeh)
  Rationale: This invites him for today, changing the agreed day.

Retry hint: The invitation needs tomorrow.

### 4. Confirm the meal from the fresh dialogue: rice and salad at seven.

شو نعمل بكرا؟

Shū niʿmal bukra?

What shall we make tomorrow?

- **A** نعمل رز وسلطة الساعة ستة. — Niʿmal ruzz w-salaṭa is-sāʿa sitte. — We’ll make rice and salad at six.
  Rationale: This keeps the dishes but imports six from the previous lesson.
- **B** نعمل شاي الساعة سبعة. — Niʿmal shāy is-sāʿa sabʿa. — We’ll make tea at seven.
  Rationale: Tea comes afterwards; it is not the meal being confirmed.
- **C (accepted)** نعمل رز وسلطة الساعة سبعة. — Niʿmal ruzz w-salaṭa is-sāʿa sabʿa. — We’ll make rice and salad at seven.
  Rationale: Both dishes and the new time match the dialogue.

Retry hint: Keep both the foods and the new clock time.

### 5. Fattoush asks for the drink mentioned after the meal. Ask Knafeh politely for that drink.

بدك شاي ولا قهوة؟

Biddik shāy walla ʾahwe?

Do you want tea or coffee? (to Fattoush)

- **A** قهوة، لو سمحتي. — ʾAhwe, law samaḥti. — Coffee, please. (to Fattoush)
  Rationale: This selects coffee and directs the request to Fattoush.
- **B (accepted)** شاي، لو سمحت. — Shāy, law samaḥt. — Tea, please. (to Knafeh)
  Rationale: Tea matches the new dialogue, and law samaḥt addresses Knafeh.
- **C** لا، شكراً. — Laʾ, shukran. — No, thank you.
  Rationale: This refuses the drink rather than requesting the one in the plan.

Retry hint: Recall the last turn of the dialogue.

### 6. Knafeh wants to know how Fattoush’s day went too. Return the check-in to Fattoush after answering.

يومي كان هادي.

Yōmi kān hādi.

My day was quiet.

- **A (accepted)** وإنتِ؟ — W-inti? — And you? (to Fattoush)
  Rationale: W-inti returns the check-in to Fattoush without introducing a different topic.
- **B** وإنتَ؟ — W-inta? — And you? (to Knafeh)
  Rationale: This addresses a man; Fattoush is the listener.
- **C** وكيفها؟ — W-kīfha? — And how is she?
  Rationale: This asks about a woman already mentioned rather than directly returning the check-in.

Retry hint: Address Fattoush directly.

### 7. Closing exchange, part 1: in a new conversation, you worked and then rested. Tell that story without changing the order.

شو عملت اليوم؟

Shū ʿmilt il-yōm?

What did you do today? (to Knafeh)

- **A** ارتحت وبعدين اشتغلت. — Irtaḥt w-baʿdēn ishtaghalt. — I rested and then worked.
  Rationale: This reverses the requested order.
- **B** زرت أمي وبعدين ارتحت. — Zurt immi w-baʿdēn irtaḥt. — I visited my mother and then rested.
  Rationale: This repeats Fattoush’s earlier story instead of the new work scenario.
- **C (accepted)** اشتغلت وبعدين ارتحت. — Ishtaghalt w-baʿdēn irtaḥt. — I worked and then rested.
  Rationale: You reuse the sequence pattern for work followed by rest, rather than copying Fattoush’s visit story.

Retry hint: Check the activity before baʿdēn.

### 8. Closing exchange, part 2: confirm the whole new arrangement: tomorrow at seven, a shared meal, then tea.

ناكل سوا بكرا الساعة سبعة؟

Nākol sawa bukra is-sāʿa sabʿa?

Shall we eat together tomorrow at seven?

- **A** تمام، اليوم الساعة سبعة. وبعدين نشرب شاي. — Tamām, il-yōm is-sāʿa sabʿa. W-baʿdēn nishrab shāy. — Okay, today at seven. And afterwards we’ll drink tea.
  Rationale: Only the day changes, but that would create a different arrangement.
- **B (accepted)** تمام، بكرا الساعة سبعة. وبعدين نشرب شاي. — Tamām, bukra is-sāʿa sabʿa. W-baʿdēn nishrab shāy. — Okay, tomorrow at seven. And afterwards we’ll drink tea.
  Rationale: The confirmation preserves the day and time and adds the requested follow-on activity.
- **C** تمام، بكرا الساعة ستة. وبعدين نشرب قهوة. — Tamām, bukra is-sāʿa sitte. W-baʿdēn nishrab ʾahwe. — Okay, tomorrow at six. And afterwards we’ll drink coffee.
  Rationale: This changes both the time and the drink.

Retry hint: Check day, time, and the activity afterwards.
