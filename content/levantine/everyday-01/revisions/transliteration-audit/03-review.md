## Dialogue

**Knafeh → Fattoush**

بدك شاي ولا قهوة؟

Biddik shāy walla ʾahwe?

Do you want tea or coffee? (to Fattoush)

**Fattoush → Knafeh**

شاي، لو سمحت.

Shāy, law samaḥt.

Tea, please. (to Knafeh)

**Knafeh → Fattoush**

بدك سكر؟

Biddik sukkar?

Do you want sugar?

**Fattoush**

لا، بدون سكر، شكراً.

Laʾ, bidūn sukkar, shukran.

No, without sugar, thank you.

**Knafeh**

أنا بدي قهوة.

Ana biddi ʾahwe.

I want coffee.

**Fattoush**

بحب الشاي أكتر.

Baḥibb ish-shāy aktar.

I like tea more.

## Teaching cards

### Offer a real choice

بدك شاي ولا قهوة؟

Biddik shāy walla ʾahwe?

Do you want tea or coffee? (to Fattoush)

Biddik addresses Fattoush. Walla offers alternatives: tea or coffee. Biddak is the form for Knafeh.

Recall: You are Knafeh. Offer Fattoush tea or coffee.

- بدك · biddik · do you want · to Fattoush
- شاي · shāy · tea
- ولا · walla · or
- قهوة · ʾahwe · coffee

Sources: https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/palestine-lexicon/lexicon, https://media.lingualism.com/wp-content/uploads/Sample-Palestinian-Arabic-Verbs-Lingualism.pdf, https://resources.lingualism.com/levantine-arabic/food-and-drink-3/, internal:ismi/everyday-01/03/guided-teaching

### Accept politely

شاي، لو سمحت.

Shāy, law samaḥt.

Tea, please. (to Knafeh)

Name the drink, then add law samaḥt when asking Knafeh. The polite ending follows your listener. In law, pronounce aw as in English “how”. Keep samaḥt for a male listener and samaḥti for a female listener.

Recall: You are Fattoush. Ask Knafeh for tea politely.

- شاي · shāy · tea
- لو سمحت · law samaḥt · please · to Knafeh

Sources: https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/palestine-lexicon/lexicon, https://media.lingualism.com/wp-content/uploads/Sample-Palestinian-Arabic-Verbs-Lingualism.pdf, https://resources.lingualism.com/levantine-arabic/food-and-drink-3/, internal:ismi/everyday-01/03/guided-teaching, https://palweb.app/library/terms/conjunction-law

### Say how you want it

لا، بدون سكر، شكراً.

Laʾ, bidūn sukkar, shukran.

No, without sugar, thank you.

Laʾ answers the sugar question. Bidūn sukkar specifies without sugar; shukran closes warmly. Laʾ, shukran can decline the drink itself when that is what was offered.

Recall: Knafeh asks about sugar. Say no sugar and thank him.

- لا · laʾ · no
- بدون · bidūn · without
- سكر · sukkar · sugar
- شكراً · shukran · thank you

Sources: https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/palestine-lexicon/lexicon, https://resources.lingualism.com/levantine-arabic/food-and-drink-3/, internal:ismi/everyday-01/03/guided-teaching

### Give your preference

بحب الشاي أكتر.

Baḥibb ish-shāy aktar.

I like tea more.

Baḥibb says what you like. Aktar adds more, comparing tea with the coffee in this scene.

Recall: Knafeh asks why you chose tea. Say you like it more.

- بحب · baḥibb · I like
- الشاي · ish-shāy · tea · the tea
- أكتر · aktar · more

Sources: https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/palestine-lexicon/lexicon, https://resources.lingualism.com/levantine-arabic/food-and-drink-3/, internal:ismi/everyday-01/03/guided-teaching

### Ask for yourself

بدي شاي.

Biddi shāy.

I want tea.

Biddi means I want. Changing biddik to biddi changes a question about Fattoush into your own request.

Recall: You are Fattoush. Say that you yourself want tea.

- بدي · biddi · I want
- شاي · shāy · tea

Sources: https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/palestine-lexicon/lexicon, https://media.lingualism.com/wp-content/uploads/Sample-Palestinian-Arabic-Verbs-Lingualism.pdf, https://resources.lingualism.com/levantine-arabic/food-and-drink-3/, internal:ismi/everyday-01/03/guided-teaching

### Change the host

قهوة، لو سمحتي.

ʾAhwe, law samaḥti.

Coffee, please. (to Fattoush)

Now Fattoush is serving Knafeh. Law samaḥti addresses Fattoush; the drink does not decide that ending. In law, pronounce aw as in English “how”. Keep samaḥt for a male listener and samaḥti for a female listener.

Recall: You are Knafeh. Fattoush is hosting. Ask her for coffee politely.

- قهوة · ʾahwe · coffee
- لو سمحتي · law samaḥti · please · to Fattoush
