# Owner session script

Use with the [pilot scope](README.md) and [observation sheet](observation-sheet.md).
All tasks below are proposed facilitator prompts, not new published exercises or
automatically graded answer records. Do not load them into the curriculum store.

## October 3 published release and session gate

Owner-selected setup: one adult false beginner on **iPhone/Safari**. Use the
[October 3 candidate](../../content/levantine/everyday-01/revisions/pilot-release/README.md)
and its manifest as the baseline: all seven are now published as hosted v2. The tasks below have been aligned
to that wording; they are not authorization to run against the old hosted v1 unit.
Before a learner session, record the target's seven published versions and verify
they match the owner-approved candidate. If the versions differ, reconcile first.
See [release/preflight evidence](release-2026-10-03/README.md).

The check-in revision follows the owner's confirmed casual-greeting intent in
LEV-2026-09-30-01. This is not a finding that the original day question is never
used. The revised task 1 and delayed task replace the old day-description tasks.
No new native-speaker review or pronunciation assessment is claimed.

## Before the participant arrives

Complete the [preflight](preflight.md). Use a separate normal browser profile per
participant, with the same profile for both sessions; private browsing may not
preserve offline data. Keep the owner's account and console out of the session.
Open the Courses page on the development preview and locate **Everyday conversations
with someone you love**. Confirm all seven lesson titles against the candidate manifest and release record.
Use the Open control for each lesson, bypassing the demonstration unit.
Record the deployed app revision if known (otherwise “unknown”), lesson IDs/versions,
browser/OS, and input or assistive technology. Do not clear the participant's cache
between sessions or use real account credentials for this guest pilot.

## Opening — read aloud or provide as text

“We’re trying out seven short Arabic lessons and testing the lessons and app.
You can take breaks, use help, skip any task, or stop. You can speak, type Arabic,
or use transliteration. We won’t record audio or video. With your agreement, I’ll
keep anonymous written notes about what worked and what was confusing, using the
storage and deletion arrangement we discussed. The lessons have owner approval,
but have not had native-expert review. Device speech is a preview.”

Confirm agreement before taking notes. Ask what they have studied before and
whether they can already ask basic questions or directions. Use broad descriptions;
do not ask for identifying relationship or family details.

## For each lesson

1. Say: “Open [title] and work through it as you normally would. Use any help you
   want. Tell me if something is confusing.” Start observer timing on opening;
   stop at completion. Log pauses separately; no visible timer or time limit.
2. Observe the conversation, phrase teaching, recall/reveal, phrase reconstruction,
   translation help, mistaken answers, feedback, retries, and help requests. Let the learner try before intervening. If assistance is needed,
   record the exact action or language hint. Do not seed an incorrect answer in
   the participant's study; the preflight separately exercises that path.
3. Ask: “Was any explanation confusing or different from what you expected?”
   Record the step ID, chosen option, feedback, and a brief anonymized paraphrase.
   A plausible dialect variant is a review question, not automatically an error.
4. Close the lesson and hide the app's answer choices. Present the corresponding
   English task below as text or aloud. Say: “Try expressing this in Arabic using
   what you remember. You can speak or write it. You can ask for help or skip.”
5. Record first unaided response before offering help. Then allow notes or a hint,
   logging the support separately. Do not treat a repeated model answer as recall.
   A participant who needs interface access assistance can still demonstrate
   unaided language recall; record these two kinds of assistance separately.

## Fresh recall tasks — learner sees only the task column

All IDs begin `levantine-everyday-01-`. The facilitator references point into the
October 3 candidate packages, pending final owner approval/publication. They constrain the intended meaning; they are
not an exhaustive list of acceptable Arabic. The owner should mark uncertain
language judgments “review needed,” preserving a short non-identifying response
only with agreement. Do not score accent or require one transliteration spelling.

| Lesson / suffix | Task to show the learner | Facilitator meaning check and reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check in with someone you love / 01 | You are Knafeh. Fattoush asks how you are doing. Say you are a little tired, then ask her how she is doing. | First-person tired + a little; asks Fattoush back with feminine address. Candidate steps e01-05 and e01-08, teaching cards “Answer about yourself” / “Keep the conversation going.” Do not require one exact wording. |
| What did you do today? / 02 | Today you rested, then visited your mother. Tell someone those two events in that order. | Rest precedes visiting own mother. See 02-review dialogue and teaching note for the first-person forms and sequence. |
| Want tea or coffee? / 03 | You are Fattoush asking Knafeh for coffee, politely, with no sugar. | Coffee, polite request to Knafeh, no sugar. See 03-review dialogue for request/address and no sugar; closing exchange for coffee. |
| Let's eat together / 04 | You are Fattoush. Say you are hungry, then suggest making rice and salad together. | Feminine self-description, shared suggestion, both dishes. See 04-review dialogue and teaching note. |
| How's your family? / 05 | You are talking to Knafeh. Ask how his family is, then ask how his sister is. | Listener's family; follow-up specifically about his sister. See 05-review dialogue turns 1 and 3. |
| What are we doing tomorrow? / 06 | Suggest drinking tea together tomorrow at six. | Shared tea activity, tomorrow, six. See 06-review dialogue/teaching note and closing exchange; recombines its time and activity. |
| Put it together / 07 | Say that today you rested and then worked. Confirm a shared meal tomorrow at seven, followed by tea. | Rest before work; tomorrow; seven; meal then tea. See 07-review practice 7–8; reverses the activity order. |

A semantic observation is not certification of grammatical accuracy. Mark each
meaning component observed/missing/uncertain, and record whether the learner used
no language help, a hint, or a visible model. Skipped and not attempted are separate
from missing. Compare supported and unaided attempts without turning them into XP.

## Session boundary and delayed recall

After lesson 3, ask what felt useful, what felt confusing, and which expression
they would use with someone they know. Do not request a personal story.
At the start of session 2, before opening lessons, ask: “You are Fattoush. Greet Knafeh with a casual check-in,
then ask whether he wants to rest.” Log this separately as delayed recall of
lesson 1. Record elapsed days and any intervening practice; it is not a controlled
retention experiment. Then proceed through lessons 4–7 with breaks as needed.

## Offline task and wrap-up

After a lesson has been completed and cached online, ask the learner to reopen it
in Practice offline, reload, complete it, reconnect, and check that progress is
still present. Explain the network change before doing it. Use the preflight's
new-completion test separately to verify queued credit; practice intentionally adds
no credit. If unavailable offline, record that result and restore connectivity.

Ask: “What would you change first? Which task was hardest to express without
choices? Would you want to use another lesson?” Record behavior separately from
opinion. Thank them, remind them how to request deletion of their notes, and stop.
