## Pronunciation-help and reading recommendation

Use an explicitly versioned **Ismi draft pronunciation help** field, optional
throughout the course, and let the fluent reviewer settle a single convention
against recordings. IPA is a credible reference direction, supported by the PC
orientation; [MSU's introduction](https://openbooks.lib.msu.edu/basickhmer/front-matter/introducation/)
explicitly calls its help IPA-adapted and warns it differs from other sources.
Do not label a mixed respelling system “IPA,” use pronunciation help as Khmer
orthography, or grade phonetics before text/help/audio alignment is checked.

For the first unit, assess recognition of the actual approved greeting, gratitude,
name, origin and repair chunks taught there. Finalize exact strings and authored
segments with the reviewer before declaring the inventory agreed. Recognizing
taught chunks is not unrestricted Khmer literacy. Keep listening, conversation and
reading results separate.

For implementation, [Unicode UAX #29](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr29/#Word_Boundaries)
requires tailored processing for reliable Khmer word boundaries; whitespace is not
a sufficient word tokenizer. Use authored segments for tiles. [W3C's September 4,
2026 Khmer gap draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/DNOTE-khmr-gap-20260904/) is a browser/layout
check reference, not a linguistic authority or a passed accessibility test.

## Verification and next dispositions

The primary teaching sources establish useful candidates; they do not complete
F26.1. Before publication, record:

1. A named fluent Cambodian Khmer reviewer's scope and reference-speaker profile,
   including ability to review Phnom Penh contemporary usage, address and culture.
2. Exact source/version/location and rights disposition for each text/helper/audio
   asset. Research access, redistribution permission and linguistic review are
   separate states.
3. A text/help/audio/segment disposition for every new utterance and variant.
4. Original permissioned human recordings, accepted-answer rationales and an
   owner approval through the console.

Reviewer checklist: compare historical FSI forms with contemporary Phnom Penh
usage; verify peer versus elder/formal address choices in every scene; verify each
phonemic-to-script conversion against an independent lexical reference; check
every pronunciation-help string against the approved recording; disposition
orthographic/code-point variants explicitly; verify chunks/tiles without adding
artificial spaces; check accepted alternatives and contextual rejection reasons.
An engineering validator must not mark these checks as completed by a human.

Additional lexical evidence located in this pass is limited: NIU unit vocabulary
contains និយាយ (speaking) and the pronoun grammar contains ខ្ញុំ (first-person
reference). [MSU 6.6](https://openbooks.lib.msu.edu/basickhmer/chapter/6-6-grammar-point-and-useful-expressions/)
supplies pronunciation help for the repeat expression but not Khmer script in the
HTML table. [NIU word list 4](https://seasite.niu.edu/khmer/vocabulary/wlist/wordlist4/wlist4.htm)
lists យឺត with a lateness gloss, so it alone does not establish a natural
slower-speech request. No inspected primary dictionary entry independently
verified the complete strings for coming-from, repetition or the capital name;
keep those compositions on the review checklist rather than describing this as
a completed dictionary audit.

No reviewer was contacted, no paid resources were acquired, and no curriculum or
media was published. Reference downloads stay in temporary storage; no original
book/media copy was added to the repository.
